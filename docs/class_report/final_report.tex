Does SAR-Domain Pretraining Outperform\\[2pt]

\vspace{0.8em}
{\large John Roth \qquad Kyle Wagner\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
{\small Johns Hopkins University \,\textbullet\, EN.705.643 Deep Learning

\begin{abstract}
Detecting dark vessels---vessels lacking matched Automatic Identification
System reports---in spaceborne synthetic aperture radar (SAR) supports
maritime safety and enforcement, but human-verified examples are scarce. We
study whether source-domain pretraining improves label efficiency on
xView3-SAR, and whether the effect persists across architectures. Two
size-matched tracks (ViT-B/16 and ConvNeXt-V2-Base) each compare random
initialization with released ImageNet, optical remote-sensing, and SAR
checkpoints under one detector, optimizer, schedule, scene-level split, and
seed. Fine-tuning nested 10--100\% fractions of 111 training scenes yields 32
controlled cells, all completed on H100 GPUs in strict FP32 and re-derived at
build time from hash-bound evidence. Transfer helps most under label
scarcity, but the SAR-versus-optical contrast is architecture-dependent: SAR
pretraining dominates optical in the CNN track at every budget while the ViT
track prefers the optical checkpoint below half data, and generic ImageNet
initialization leads both tracks at full data. Reported values are corrected
development-selection F1\ifHevTestComplete{} alongside once-scored held-out
test results\fi.\ifHevFinalEval\else{} The 50-scene human-verified
evaluation remains sealed for follow-on work.\fi
\end{abstract}
