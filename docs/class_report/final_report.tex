\begin{abstract}
Dark vessels lack a matched Automatic Identification System report, which
makes them harder to identify and monitor. Spaceborne synthetic aperture
radar (SAR) observes through cloud and darkness, but human-verified examples
remain scarce. We measure how pretraining source changes label efficiency on
xView3-SAR and whether the ranking persists across backbone families. Two
approximately size-matched tracks use ViT-B/16 and ConvNeXt-V2-Base. Within
each track, random, ImageNet, optical remote-sensing, and SAR initializations
share one detector, training recipe, scene split, and seed. We fine-tuned four
nested budgets from 12 to 111 training scenes, for 32 controlled cells. On
corrected development-selection F1, every pretrained ViT and two of three
pretrained CNNs beat their random floors at 12 scenes. The SAR--optical
ranking depended on the backbone: SAR led at every CNN budget, while optical
led the ViT track at 10\% and 25\%.\ifHevTestComplete{} The once-scored
16-scene test split preserved these rankings. At full data, development
selection favored ImageNet in both tracks, but test scoring favored SAR over
ImageNet in the ViT track. Two SAR test curves exceeded the predeclared 0.02
non-monotonicity tolerance, so the experiment failed its monotonicity
gate.\else{} Held-out test scoring remains pending.\fi{} All values are
seed-0 point estimates.\ifHevFinalEval{} The once-only 50-scene evaluation
supplies dark-vessel recall and near-shore performance.\else{} The 50
human-verified scenes remain sealed, so this report does not yet measure
dark-vessel recall or near-shore performance.\fi
\end{abstract}
