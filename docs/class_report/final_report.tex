Does SAR-Domain Pretraining Outperform\\[2pt]

\vspace{0.8em}
{\large John Roth \qquad Kyle Wagner\par}
\vspace{0.25em}
{\small Johns Hopkins University \,\textbullet\, EN.705.643 Deep Learning
Developments with PyTorch \,\textbullet\, August 2026\par}

\begin{abstract}
Dark vessels operate without a matched Automatic Identification System
report. Spaceborne synthetic aperture radar (SAR) can find them through
cloud and darkness, but human-verified examples are scarce. We test whether
source-domain pretraining reduces the labeled scenes a detector needs on
xView3-SAR, and whether the effect persists across architectures. Two
size-matched tracks use ViT-B/16 and ConvNeXt-V2-Base backbones. Within
each track, four arms compare random initialization with released ImageNet,
optical remote-sensing, and SAR checkpoints under one detector, optimizer,
schedule, scene-level split, and seed. We fine-tuned nested 10--100\%
fractions of 111 training scenes, which gives 32 controlled cells. All 32
cells completed on H100 GPUs in strict FP32, and the build re-derives every
published value from hash-bound evidence. Transfer helps most when labels
are scarce. The SAR-versus-optical contrast depends on the architecture:
SAR pretraining beats optical in the CNN track at every budget, but optical
leads in the ViT track below half data. Generic ImageNet initialization
leads both tracks at full data. Reported values are corrected
development-selection F1\ifHevTestComplete{} alongside once-scored held-out
test results\fi.\ifHevFinalEval\else{} The 50-scene human-verified
evaluation remains sealed for follow-on work.\fi
\end{abstract}
