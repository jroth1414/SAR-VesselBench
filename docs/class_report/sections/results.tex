\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Execution summary and reporting contract.}

The core result is one complete cohort: eight arms at four nested budgets,
seed 0, all 32 cells finished on one \HevHardware{} class in strict FP32
(\HevGPUHours{} GPU-hours; code \texttt{\HevCodeSHAShort}; cohort
\texttt{\HevCohortSHAShort}). The build re-derives every published value
from the committed evidence: the frozen cohort, the hash-bound completion
markers, and the full training curves. A single inconsistent byte stops the
build. The reported metric is corrected development-selection F1. Each cell
selects its checkpoint and operating threshold on the eight development
scenes, so the values carry selection optimism and are not held-out
estimates. Table~\ref{tab:dev-matrix} lists the matrix;
Fig.~\ref{fig:label-efficiency} shows the four-point curves. Training
dynamics appear in Appendix~\ref{app:figures}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:training-dynamics}).

\paragraph{Label-efficiency curves and transfer gains.}

The two tracks occupy different regimes. Every pretrained ViT arm clears
its random floor at 12 scenes: optical by \HevDeltaFTenViTOptical, SAR by
\HevDeltaFTenViTSar, and ImageNet by \HevDeltaFTenViTImageNet{}
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:gain}; gain panels in Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-gains},
Appendix~\ref{app:figures}). The CNN track spreads wider. SAR gains
\HevDeltaFTenCNNSar{} and ImageNet \HevDeltaFTenCNNImageNet{} over the
12-scene floor. The optical Sentinel-2 arm only matches its floor at 12
scenes (both \HevDevFCNNOpticalTen) and falls below it at 25\% and 50\%:
the land-cover source gives this backbone features that fine-tuning must
first undo.

Two curves violate monotonicity at the 10\%--25\% transition: ViT-optical
drops from \HevDevFViTOpticalTen{} to \HevDevFViTOpticalTwentyFive{} and
ViT-SAR from \HevDevFViTSarTen{} to \HevDevFViTSarTwentyFive. We report
these declines as observed, and we do not smooth or rerun them. The metric
rests on a few hundred positives over eight fixed scenes, so a shift of a
few hundredths corresponds to a handful of detections at the swept
threshold. The protocol resolves such selection noise with held-out scoring
on the sealed sixteen-scene split.

We also ask for the smallest tested budget at which an arm reaches its
track's full-data random floor. CNN-ImageNet crosses at 28 scenes
(\HevDevFCNNImageNetTwentyFive{} against \HevDevFCNNRandomHundred). Every
ViT transfer arm and CNN-SAR cross at 56 scenes. CNN-optical never crosses.
No arm crosses at 12 scenes: the strongest 12-scene cell, ViT-optical at
\HevDevFViTOpticalTen, sits just below the ViT floor's
\HevDevFViTRandomHundred. Under this development-selection metric, a
well-chosen public checkpoint at 12 scenes nearly, but not fully,
substitutes for 111 random-initialization scenes.

\paragraph{Source-domain contrasts and architecture generality.}

The CNN track orders SAR above optical at every budget
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:contrast}; right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-gains}),
with the largest contrast under scarcity (\HevDevFCNNSarTen{} versus
\HevDevFCNNOpticalTen{} at 12 scenes). The BigEarthNet Sentinel-1 and
Sentinel-2 checkpoints share architecture, dataset family, and supervised
task, so this is the study's cleanest modality effect. The ViT track shows
the opposite order below half data: optical leads SAR at 10\% and 25\%
(\HevDevFViTOpticalTen{} versus \HevDevFViTSarTen;
\HevDevFViTOpticalTwentyFive{} versus \HevDevFViTSarTwentyFive), and SAR
moves ahead only from 56 scenes upward. The sign of the SAR--optical
contrast does not replicate across architectures under label scarcity. A
modality-matched encoder is not always the best scarce-label choice.
Architecture and pretraining objective moderate the effect, and the
released ViT checkpoints also differ in objective and source volume, so
their contrast describes available checkpoints rather than modality alone.

At full data, generic ImageNet initialization leads both tracks
(\HevDevFViTImageNetHundred{} and \HevDevFCNNImageNetHundred). Domain
pretraining matters most where labels are scarce. With enough target data,
generic visual structure plus full fine-tuning closes the domain gap.

\paragraph{Held-out results.}

\ifHevTestComplete
All 32 cells were scored once on the frozen 16-scene test split with their
cohort-bound development thresholds (inference precision
\HevTestInferencePrecision). The mean development-to-test F1 change is
\HevTestMeanGap; the full test column appears in
Appendix~\ref{app:inventory}. Development selection carries optimism from
choosing both checkpoint and threshold on eight scenes, so the test column
is the honest operating estimate.
\else
We did not score the 16-scene test split before this build. The held-out
contract freezes the 32-cell cohort first, applies each cell's
development-bound threshold verbatim, and writes one immutable result per
cell. The generator admits the test column only when all 32 results
validate. We scored two cross-hardware control cells locally under this
contract (RTX 5070 Ti, strict FP32). Their development-to-test changes were
$-0.080$ and $-0.069$, consistent with development-selection optimism
rather than threshold-transfer failure.
\fi
\ifHevFinalEval
The sealed 50-scene human-verified evaluation ran once for the 24 cells at
the 10\%, 25\%, and 100\% budgets; dark-vessel recall and near-shore
results appear in Appendix~\ref{app:inventory}.
\else
The 50 human-verified scenes remain sealed. The protocol defines
dark-vessel recall and near-shore performance only on that set, which
awaits its once-only evaluation.
\fi
