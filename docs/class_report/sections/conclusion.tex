\section{Conclusion and Future Work}

Five of the six pretrained checkpoints beat their random floors at 12
scenes, but no source won across both backbones and both selection stages.
CNN-SAR also leads CNN-optical at every budget. The ViT track gives a
different scarce-label result: optical leads SAR at 10\% and 25\%, then SAR
leads at the two larger budgets.\ifHevTestComplete{} The held-out test split
preserves these SAR--optical rankings. At 111 scenes, development selection
ranks ImageNet first in both tracks, while test scoring ranks SAR above
ImageNet for ViT.\fi

\ifHevTestComplete
The failed monotonicity gate limits these conclusions. Two SAR test curves
decline by more than 0.02 as the label budget grows, including a 50\%--100\%
drop that development selection misses. The complete grid is therefore a
set of descriptive seed-0 point estimates, not evidence that performance
improves monotonically with more labels.
\else
The frozen 16-scene test split remains the next held-out stage. It will use
each cell's development-bound threshold without retuning.
\fi

The design adds further limits. Each cell uses one seed, checkpoint and
threshold selection uses eight scenes, and the 16-scene test split measures
in-region generalization. The ViT optical and SAR checkpoints also differ in
objective and source corpus. The results support comparisons among these
released checkpoints under one detector recipe. They do not identify a
universal causal effect of pretraining domain.

The once-only 50-scene human-verified evaluation supplies dark-vessel recall

\fi
