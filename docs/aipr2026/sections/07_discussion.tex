\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The controlled comparisons answer two linked questions: whether a downloaded
SAR-domain initialization improves label-efficient vessel detection within
each architecture, and whether the direction of that within-track contrast
persists across ViT and CNN backbones.

On the first question, the campaign's development evidence splits the answer
by comparison. Against optical pretraining, the SAR checkpoint wins in both
tracks at all four label fractions; the CNN version of that contrast, the
design's cleanest modality comparison (Sect.~\ref{sec:design}), separates
the sources by \SarOptCnnHundredAbs{} to \SarOptCnnTwentyFiveAbs. Against
generic ImageNet transfer, however, SAR pretraining loses in seven of eight
track--fraction cells. The title question resolves to: SAR-domain
pretraining beats optical transfer, and nothing in this campaign beats
ImageNet.

On the second question, the sign of the SAR--optical contrast replicates
across architecture families at every fraction, while its magnitude does
not. The CNN track separates sources by up to \SarOptCnnTwentyFiveAbs{}
where the ViT track compresses them to \SarOptVitMaxAbs{} or less. One
reading is that the ViT arms sit close to a performance level reachable from
random initialization on this task, which leaves little headroom for any
source domain to matter. The CNN random floor starts much lower at small
budgets, so the CNN track exposes initialization quality. The negative
transfer of the optical Sentinel-2 CNN checkpoint, below its own random
floor at every fraction, is a caution against assuming that overhead imagery
of any modality helps a SAR task. Land-cover optical features appear to be a
poor starting point for detecting vessel-scale bright returns.

\subsection{Why does generic pretraining win?}
\label{sec:mechanisms}

Three readings are consistent with the observed pattern, and the design
separates them only partly. The first is feature generality at scale. Both
ImageNet controls distill 1.28 million natural images under strong
augmentation, and after decibel scaling and per-channel normalization a
vessel is a compact bright blob against textured clutter: detecting it
rests on generic low- and mid-level features (edges, blobs, local contrast)
that large-scale natural-image pretraining supplies in abundance. A
domain-specific corpus must beat that supply with relevance, and in this
campaign only the SAR checkpoints come close.

The second reading is objective mismatch, made concrete by the negative
transfer of the optical CNN arm. BigEarthNet training optimizes multi-label
land-cover classification over whole patches, which rewards aggregate
texture and spectral statistics and exerts no pressure to preserve
point-target detail; those features start the detector below its own random
floor. The matched Sentinel-1 checkpoint shares this objective yet
transfers well, so modality match can outweigh objective mismatch, but not
by enough to overtake ImageNet.

The third reading is headroom. The ViT random floor is already high on the
development support, compressing every pretraining effect to
\SarOptVitMaxAbs{} or less, while the CNN floor starts low and exposes
initialization quality. Within that compressed ViT band, SARMAE's masked
autoencoding on SAR-1M, a reconstruction objective that preserves
small-target structure, supplies the one cell where a domain checkpoint
beats ImageNet (\DevFVitSarTwentyFive{} against
\DevFVitImageNetTwentyFive{} at 28 scenes). These are readings, not
conclusions: one seed, development-selection scores, and released-checkpoint
confounds (Sect.~\ref{sec:limitations}) all apply, and the held-out matrix
with seed replication is the designed test.

\subsection{Limits of the comparison}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Development-selection scores.}
The reported values are best-development F1 scores on the fixed eight-scene
development subset, computed under the training-time label conversion that
the audit later corrected (Sect.~\ref{sec:gt-contract}), with per-cell
threshold sweeps on the same subset that drives early stopping. They are
selection scores, biased upward relative to held-out scoring, and the
declining ViT curves show the selection surface is not a monotone function
of training-set size. Section~\ref{sec:gt-contract} explains why between-arm
comparisons remain internally consistent while the absolute values are not
exchangeable with held-out scores. The frozen checkpoints and the staged
test view make the deferred held-out matrix a mechanical follow-on step.

\paragraph{One fixed seed.}
All 32 controlled cells use seed~0. The curves are point estimates, not
estimates of run-to-run variability. We report no error bars, confidence
intervals, or statistical significance tests, and small fraction-wise
differences should not be overinterpreted.

\paragraph{In-region rather than geographic holdout.}
The splits are disjoint by scene identifier, but Sentinel-1 revisits create
geographic proximity across splits: 22 of 23 development scenes fall within
32~km of a training scene, and 42 of the 50 human-verified scenes fall within
50~km of a training scene. The reported metrics characterize in-region
behavior under scene-level separation, not transfer to unseen geographies or
acquisition regimes.

\paragraph{Source domain is not isolated from pretraining recipe.}
The ViT comparison pairs SatDINO, trained with a DINO objective on optical
fMoW imagery, with SARMAE, trained by masked autoencoding on SAR-1M. Their
difference includes objective and corpus construction as well as modality.
The CNN BigEarthNet Sentinel-2/Sentinel-1 pair is the cleaner modality
contrast because model family, downstream source task, and dataset family are
matched more closely. The generic controls also have different histories:
the ViT checkpoint uses supervised AugReg on ImageNet-1K, whereas the
ConvNeXt-V2 checkpoint uses FCMAE pretraining followed by supervised
ImageNet-1K fine-tuning. We therefore make domain claims within architecture
and treat cross-track agreement as replication of a pattern, not a pooled
causal estimate of source modality.

\paragraph{Scope of the controlled comparison.}
ViT-B/16 and ConvNeXt-V2-Base are approximately size matched, but their
pretraining data volume, optimization compute, feature geometry, and
training histories differ, and their detector adapters are necessarily
family-specific; matched-role ViT--CNN gaps therefore include architecture,
adapter, and pretraining-history effects that the measured GPU-hours and
parameter counts expose but do not remove. Numerically, every cell ran
strict FP32 on one V100 with TF32 disabled, which removes precision as a
within-study variable but does not establish that identical rankings would
hold under other formats or hardware. The study covers one dataset, one
point-detector family, four tested label budgets, and one seed; broader
claims require additional sensors, regions, detectors, and seeds.
