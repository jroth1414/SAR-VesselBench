\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We executed a controlled, two-track experiment measuring how random,
ImageNet, optical remote-sensing, and SAR initializations affect
label-efficient dark-vessel detection on xView3: 32 cells covering eight
size-matched arms at four nested scene budgets, under one detector, one
recipe, one seed, and strict FP32. On the recorded development evidence,
initialization dominates in the scarce-label regime. Twelve labeled scenes
with a SAR or ImageNet start match or beat 111 labeled scenes from random
initialization in both architecture families. The SAR checkpoint beats the
optical checkpoint in both tracks at every fraction, with the
architecture-matched BigEarthNet Sentinel-1/Sentinel-2 pair isolating the
modality effect in the CNN track, where optical land-cover pretraining
transfers worse than no pretraining at all. Generic ImageNet initialization
remains the strongest overall start, leading in seven of eight
track--fraction cells.

These results delimit what one controlled seed-0 comparison can say: the
direction of the source-domain effect replicates across two architecture
families while its magnitude is architecture-dependent. The reported values
are development-selection scores, and the protocol's sealed stages remain
ahead: scoring the frozen checkpoints on the 16-scene test matrix under the
corrected contract, the once-only 50-scene human-verified evaluation with
its dark-vessel recall and near-shore slices, and seed replication to bound
run-to-run variance.
