\subsection{Label-efficiency curves}
\label{sec:label-efficiency-results}

For track $t\in\{\mathrm{ViT},\mathrm{CNN}\}$, initialization role $r$, and
tested label fraction $f\in\mathcal{F}=\{10,25,50,100\}\%$, let
$F_{r,f}^{(t)}$ denote held-out test F1 at the dev-selected operating point.
Table~\ref{tab:core-results} reports all 32 point estimates together with the
selected epoch, measured runtime, and GPU-hours.  The two panels of
Fig.~\ref{fig:label-efficiency} retain the architecture tracks rather than
pooling them: solid curves show the ViT-B/16 arms and dashed curves show the
ConvNeXt-V2-Base arms, with a common color for each matched initialization
role.  No uncertainty ribbon is drawn because the experiment has one fixed
seed.

We avoid estimating a continuous ``percent labels saved'' quantity after
observing only four budgets.  Instead, we give a discrete crossing summary.
For a pretrained role $r$, define
\begin{equation}
  B_{r}^{(t)} =
  \min\left\{f\in\mathcal{F}:F_{r,f}^{(t)}
  \geq F_{\mathrm{random},100}^{(t)}\right\}.
  \label{eq:discrete-crossing}
\end{equation}
Thus, $B_{r}^{(t)}$ is the smallest \emph{tested} budget at which an arm
reaches the full-data random baseline in its own track; a missing crossing is
reported as ``not reached.''  Equation~\eqref{eq:discrete-crossing} is a
descriptive grid summary, not an interpolation between fractions.

\subsection{Transfer gains and source-domain contrasts}
\label{sec:transfer-results}

We summarize transfer relative to the track-matched random floor as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{r,f}^{(t)} =
  F_{r,f}^{(t)}-F_{\mathrm{random},f}^{(t)},
  \label{eq:transfer-gain}
\end{equation}
and the within-track SAR--optical contrast as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{SAR-opt},f}^{(t)} =
  F_{\mathrm{SAR},f}^{(t)}-F_{\mathrm{optical},f}^{(t)}.
  \label{eq:sar-optical-gap}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:transfer-gains} shows these signed differences at each tested
fraction.  Positive values of Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-gain} indicate an
advantage over training the same architecture from random initialization;
positive values of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap} indicate an advantage for
the SAR checkpoint over the optical checkpoint in that architecture.  These
are within-track contrasts.  Agreement in the sign and fraction-wise pattern
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap} across tracks is the evidence used to assess
architecture generality; it is not treated as a pooled effect estimate.

\subsection{External references and human-verified evaluation}
\label{sec:final-results}

The corrected YOLO26 and LocateAnything results provide external context in
Table~\ref{tab:final-eval}.  Because their architectures, supervision, input
construction, and operating procedures differ from the controlled arms, they
remain off the curves and are not used in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:transfer-gain}
or~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap}.

After the 32-cell cohort and its checkpoint-bound thresholds are frozen, the
models are evaluated once on the disjoint 50-scene human-verified set.
Table~\ref{tab:final-eval} reports aggregate F1, dark-vessel recall, and
near-shore F1, while Fig.~\ref{fig:final-slices} visualizes the two operational
slices.  Dark-vessel recall is available only on this set; it is not
back-filled from dev or test labels.  The two valid near-shore vessel targets
in the 16-scene test set make test near-shore F1 a sparse diagnostic, so the
human-verified set supplies the substantive coastal result.
