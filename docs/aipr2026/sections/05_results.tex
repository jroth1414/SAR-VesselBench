\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Execution summary and reporting contract}
\label{sec:result-provenance}

The core result is one complete cohort of 32 runs: eight initialization arms,
four nested label fractions, and seed~0. All 32 strict-FP32 core cells and
both external references finished by \ResultCompletedUTC. Every core marker
records code revision \texttt{\ResultCodeRev}, detector hash
\texttt{\ResultDetectorHash}, Lightning \texttt{\ResultPrecision}, micro-batch
and effective batch 16, and disabled TF32. No completion marker contains a
non-finite metric. Core execution consumed \ResultCoreGPUHours{} GPU-hours;
the references consumed \ResultReferenceGPUHours{} GPU-hours, for
\ResultTotalGPUHours{} GPU-hours total. End-to-end parameter counts were
\ResultVitParameterCount{} for the ViT track and \ResultCnnParameterCount{}
for the CNN track.

Every value in Table~\ref{tab:core-results} is bound to one best-development
checkpoint and the operating threshold selected with that checkpoint, under
the single evaluation contract of Sect.~\ref{sec:gt-contract}. The reported
metric is aggregate development F1 at that operating point. The two external
references are reported separately in Table~\ref{tab:references}.

For track $t\in\{\mathrm{ViT},\mathrm{CNN}\}$, initialization role $r$, and
tested label fraction $f\in\mathcal{F}=\{10,25,50,100\}\%$, $F_{r,f}^{(t)}$
is the development F1 defined above. Table~\ref{tab:core-results} reports all
32 point estimates together with the recovered best epoch, the epochs run,
and measured GPU-hours. The two panels of
Fig.~\ref{fig:label-efficiency} retain the architecture tracks rather than
pooling them: one panel shows the ViT-B/16 arms and the other the
ConvNeXt-V2-Base arms, with a common color for each matched initialization
role.

The two tracks occupy different regimes. Every ViT arm scores between
\DevFVitRandomTwentyFive{} and \DevFVitSarTwentyFive{} at the two smallest
budgets, and every ViT arm ends below its own scarce-label peak, finishing
between \DevFVitRandomHundred{} and \DevFVitImageNetHundred{} at 111 scenes. The CNN
track spreads much wider: at 12 scenes the random floor sits at
\DevFCnnRandomTen{} while ImageNet reaches \DevFCnnImageNetTen, and the SAR
and ImageNet arms hold their advantage at every budget. ImageNet
initialization gives the highest F1 in seven of the eight track--fraction
cells; the exception is the 28-scene ViT cell, where the SAR checkpoint
reaches \DevFVitSarTwentyFive{} against \DevFVitImageNetTwentyFive{} for
ImageNet.

Several arms violate monotonicity at the 25\%--50\% transition: development
F1 drops by \MonoDropVitRandom{} for ViT random, \MonoDropVitOptical{} for
SatDINO, \MonoDropVitSar{} for SARMAE, \MonoDropVitImageNet{} for ViT
ImageNet, and \MonoDropCnnImageNet{} for CNN ImageNet. We report these
declines as observed and do not smooth or selectively rerun the curves.
Because the metric is development F1 under a fixed per-cell threshold sweep,
a larger training set does not guarantee a higher score on the fixed
eight-scene development subset.

We avoid estimating a continuous ``percent labels saved'' quantity after
observing only four budgets. Instead, we give a discrete crossing summary.
For a pretrained role $r$, define
\begin{equation}
  B_{r}^{(t)} =
  \min\left\{f\in\mathcal{F}:F_{r,f}^{(t)}
  \geq F_{\mathrm{random},100}^{(t)}\right\}.
  \label{eq:discrete-crossing}
\end{equation}
Thus, $B_{r}^{(t)}$ is the smallest \emph{tested} budget at which an arm
reaches the full-data random baseline in its own track; we report a missing
crossing as ``not reached.''  Equation~\eqref{eq:discrete-crossing} is a
descriptive grid summary, not an interpolation between fractions.

\paragraph{Label efficiency.}
In the ViT track, every pretrained arm crosses the full-data random floor of
\FloorVit{} at the smallest tested budget of 12 scenes. In the CNN track, the
SAR and ImageNet arms cross the floor of \FloorCnn{} at 12 scenes with
\DevFCnnSarTen{} and \DevFCnnImageNetTen, while the optical arm never reaches
it at any tested budget. Under this study's development-selection metric, 12
labeled scenes with a well-chosen initialization match or beat 111 labeled
scenes from random initialization in both architecture families.

\subsection{Transfer gains and source-domain contrasts}
\label{sec:transfer-results}

\input{generated/v100_fig_transfer_gains}

We summarize transfer relative to the track-matched random floor as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{r,f}^{(t)} =
  F_{r,f}^{(t)}-F_{\mathrm{random},f}^{(t)},
  \label{eq:transfer-gain}
\end{equation}
and the within-track SAR--optical contrast as
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{SAR-opt},f}^{(t)} =
  F_{\mathrm{SAR},f}^{(t)}-F_{\mathrm{optical},f}^{(t)}.
  \label{eq:sar-optical-gap}
\end{equation}
Figure~\ref{fig:transfer-gains} shows these signed differences at each tested
fraction. Positive values of Eq.~\eqref{eq:transfer-gain} indicate an
advantage over training the same architecture from random initialization;
positive values of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap} indicate an advantage for
the SAR checkpoint over the optical checkpoint in that architecture. These
are within-track contrasts. Agreement in the sign and fraction-wise pattern
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap} across tracks is the evidence used to assess
architecture generality; it is not treated as a pooled effect estimate.

\paragraph{ViT domain contrast.}
Transfer gains in the ViT track are positive but small. The SAR arm gains
between \GainVitSarTenAbs{} and \GainVitSarTwentyFiveAbs{} over the
random floor across the four budgets, the optical arm between
\GainVitOpticalTenAbs{} and \GainVitOpticalTwentyFiveAbs, and the ImageNet
arm between \GainVitImageNetFiftyAbs{} and \GainVitImageNetHundredAbs. The
SAR--optical contrast is positive at every fraction but never exceeds
\SarOptVitMaxAbs, and it shrinks as labels increase. A strong ViT baseline
from random initialization leaves little room for source-domain separation on
this task.

\paragraph{CNN domain contrast.}
The CNN track separates the sources. The SAR arm gains
\GainCnnSarTenAbs{} at 12 scenes and \GainCnnSarHundredAbs{} at 111; the
ImageNet arm gains \GainCnnImageNetTwentyFiveAbs{} at its peak. The optical
Sentinel-2 arm is the outlier: its gain is negative at every fraction,
reaching \GainCnnOpticalTwentyFive{} at 28 scenes, so land-cover optical
pretraining transferred worse than no pretraining at all. The SAR--optical
contrast runs from \SarOptCnnHundredAbs{} to \SarOptCnnTwentyFiveAbs{} and is
largest in the scarce-label regime. Because the BigEarthNet Sentinel-1 and
Sentinel-2 checkpoints share architecture, dataset family, and supervised
task, this contrast is the study's cleanest modality effect.

\paragraph{Architecture generality.}
The right panel of Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer-gains} plots the SAR--optical
contrast for both tracks. It is positive in both tracks at all four fractions:
eight of eight signed comparisons favor the SAR checkpoint. The magnitude is
architecture-dependent, at or below \SarOptVitMaxAbs{} for ViT against
\SarOptCnnHundredAbs{} to \SarOptCnnTwentyFiveAbs{} for the CNN. The direction of
the source-domain effect replicates across architecture families; its size
does not.

\subsection{External references}
\label{sec:final-results}

Table~\ref{tab:references} reports the two context systems under the same
development-scene scoring: YOLO26 fine-tuned on the full 111-scene training
split with its published recipe, and zero-shot LocateAnything-3B. Their
architectures, supervision, input construction, and operating procedures
differ from the controlled arms, so they remain off the curves and are not
used in Eqs.~\eqref{eq:transfer-gain} or~\eqref{eq:sar-optical-gap}. The
protocol's sealed stages remain ahead of this campaign: the 16-scene test
matrix, and the once-only 50-scene human-verified evaluation with its
dark-vessel and near-shore slices. We do not substitute development values
for either stage.
